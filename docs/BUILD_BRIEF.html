MamaTerryCollections — Fashion E-Commerce Platform

Build brief, MVP scope, Claude Code prompt and budget

1. Concept

A branded online store for clothes, bags, caps and related fashion items — customers browse, pick sizes/colours, pay, and track their order; the owner runs the whole business from one dashboard: stock levels, cost vs. selling price on every item, barcodes for physical stock-taking and in-person sales, and a sales team she can add, monitor and appraise. Built as a website plus an installable app, not a third-party marketplace listing — MamaTerryCollections owns its customers, its branding, and its data.

2. What Kenyan fashion e-commerce actually looks like right now

Most Kenyan clothing businesses today run through Facebook/Instagram/WhatsApp, with boda boda (motorbike) delivery and payment by M-Pesa or cash on delivery — direct buyer-seller engagement without a "real" storefront (source: African Business). That's exactly the gap a proper website + app closes: it looks and feels professional, it doesn't depend on an algorithm showing your post, and it doesn't get lost in someone's DMs.

A few hard facts worth designing around: credit card penetration in Kenya is under 5%, so M-Pesa has to be the default payment method, not an add-on — international cards decline often for fraud checks, bank transfers are slow, and cash-on-delivery carries a high return/no-show rate (source: IntaSend). Note too that mainstream platforms like Shopify don't support M-Pesa natively — stores there route through a third-party gateway (Paystack, DPO, Flutterwave, or Kenya-focused providers like IntaSend) (source: Shopify community; IntaSend). Building your own stack with Supabase and the Daraja API directly avoids that layer entirely.

Local Kenyan agencies selling "online store setup" packages standardise on: M-Pesa STK Push checkout, Kenyan shipping zones (Nairobi / counties / nationwide) often integrated with courier services like Sendy or Fargo, mobile-first design (most Kenyan shoppers are on phones), real-time inventory with low-stock alerts, customer accounts with order tracking, and SMS order notifications (source: Hostiko Kenya) — every one of those is in the MVP below. And Driplift Kenya shows the direct competitor shape: a clothing/shoes/accessories store with both a website and a Play Store app (source: Gust company profile) — confirming that customers here do expect an installable app alongside the site, not just a webpage.

3. Roles & access

  • Owner — full access: catalog, pricing, cost prices/margins, all orders, all staff, reports, settings
  • Sales Manager (owner adds these) — manages the catalog and stock for assigned categories, processes and fulfils orders, applies discounts up to an owner-set limit, views their own and their team's sales performance; cannot see other managers' cost-price/margin data unless the owner grants it
  • Sales Attendant/Cashier (for in-person sales at a physical stall/shop, if she has one) — can run the barcode "quick sale" flow, cannot edit prices or view profit margins
  • Customer — browses, buys, tracks orders, manages their own account
  • All access enforced at the backend (Row-Level Security), not just hidden in the UI

4. Core modules

Storefront (customer-facing)

  • Product catalog with categories — Clothes, Bags, Caps, plus owner-extensible categories for anything new she starts stocking (shoes, jewellery, etc. — not hardcoded)
  • Each product: multiple images, description, price, available sizes and colours (variants), stock status per variant ("In Stock" / "Low Stock" / "Sold Out")
  • Search, filter (category, size, colour, price range), and a "browse more" recommendation strip (related/same-category items) so customers keep discovering stock
  • Wishlist/save-for-later, product reviews and star ratings
  • Cart, guest checkout option, and full customer accounts (order history, saved delivery addresses, saved payment preference)

Checkout & payments — "preferred payment/selling procedures"

  • Payment methods configurable by the owner: M-Pesa STK Push (default/primary), card via Paystack/Flutterwave as a fallback, and Cash on Delivery if she wants to offer it for specific areas (with the return-rate risk noted above, so make it toggleable per delivery zone rather than blanket-on)
  • Delivery options: courier/boda delivery with configurable zone-based fees (Nairobi / other counties / nationwide, matching the local standard above), or customer pickup from a physical location if she has one
  • Order status pipeline: Placed → Confirmed → Packed → Out for Delivery/Ready for Pickup → Delivered/Collected → (Returned, if applicable)
  • Every order generates a receipt/invoice; SMS and email sent at each status change

Stock, barcodes & profit margins (owner dashboard — this is the core ask)

  • Every product variant (a specific size + colour combination) gets its own SKU and an auto-generated barcode, printable as a sticker — used both for physical stock counts and for a quick in-person "scan to sell" mode at a stall or shop counter, alongside the normal online checkout
  • Real-time stock per variant, automatically decremented by both online orders and in-person barcode sales, with low-stock alerts
  • Profit margin, calculated automatically: cost price vs. selling price per variant, gross margin %, and total profit across any date range, by product, by category, or store-wide — visible to the owner (and to sales managers only if she grants that visibility)
  • Stock valuation: total cost-basis and retail-basis value of everything currently in stock
  • Purchase/restock tracking: record new stock received, update cost price if a new batch cost differently

Sales team management & performance

  • Owner adds Sales Managers (and Sales Attendants for in-person sales), assigns them to categories or the whole catalog
  • Per-staff performance: sales value, number of orders/transactions handled, average order value, any voided/corrected sales — the same appraisal-ready record pattern used across your other builds, visible to the owner for reviews

Notifications

  • New order placed, payment received, low stock on a variant, order status needs action (e.g. ready to pack), customer review submitted, cash discrepancy (for in-person sales)

Audit log

  • Every price change, stock adjustment, discount override, and voided/refunded order logged with who did it and when — orders are voided/refunded with a reason, never silently deleted

5. Recommended stack

Same core pattern as your other builds, for consistency:

  • Web: Next.js (App Router), TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, Framer Motion — the storefront especially benefits from polish here (product image zoom/gallery transitions, smooth cart drawer, animated "added to cart" feedback), deployed on Vercel
  • App: installable PWA first (covers "website and app" without app-store overhead, same reasoning as your other lean builds); Expo/React Native later only if she specifically wants Play Store/App Store presence for marketing reasons
  • Backend: Supabase — Postgres with Row-Level Security, Auth, Storage (product images), Realtime (stock updates, order status)
  • Barcode: JsBarcode (or similar) to generate/print variant barcodes; @zxing/browser or quagga.js for in-browser camera barcode scanning in the quick-sale mode
  • Payments: M-Pesa Daraja API (STK Push) direct integration as primary; Paystack or Flutterwave for card fallback
  • Delivery: manual zone/fee configuration at launch; Sendy or Fargo API integration later if courier volume justifies it
  • Notifications: Africa's Talking (SMS), Resend (email)
  • Ops: GitHub (connected), Vercel, Supabase project already provisioned

- Notifications: Africa's Talking (SMS), Resend (email)

ROLES (Supabase Auth + RLS policies per role, enforced at the backend not just the UI)
1. owner — full access: catalog, pricing, cost prices, margins, all orders, staff, reports
2. sales_manager — manages assigned categories/stock, processes and fulfils orders, applies
   discounts up to an owner-configured limit, sees own/team performance; cost price and margin
   visibility is an owner-controlled flag per manager
3. sales_attendant — runs the in-person barcode quick-sale flow only; no pricing/margin access
4. customer — browses, buys, tracks own orders, manages own account

CORE DATA MODEL (Postgres/Supabase — adjust names as needed but keep this shape)
- categories (id, name, parent_category_id (nullable)) — OWNER-EDITABLE. Seed: Clothes, Bags,
  Caps, Other. Owner must be able to add/rename/remove at any time — do not hardcode this list.
- products (id, category_id, name, description, base_price, images: array of storage URLs,
  active, created_at)
- product_variants (id, product_id, size, colour, sku (unique), barcode_value (unique,
  auto-generated on creation), cost_price, selling_price, quantity_on_hand,
  low_stock_threshold)
- customers (id, user_id, name, phone, email, saved_addresses: jsonb)
- carts (id, customer_id or session_id, status) and cart_items (cart_id, variant_id, quantity)
- orders (id, customer_id, status: placed/confirmed/packed/out_for_delivery/delivered/returned/
  cancelled, delivery_method: courier/pickup, delivery_zone, delivery_fee, payment_method:
  mpesa/card/cod, payment_status, subtotal, total, placed_at, handled_by_staff_id (nullable,
  for in-person sales))
- order_items (id, order_id, variant_id, quantity, unit_price_at_sale, line_total)
- payments (id, order_id, amount, method, mpesa_ref (nullable), status, paid_at)
- reviews (id, product_id, customer_id, rating 1-5, comment, created_at)
- staff (id, user_id, role: sales_manager/sales_attendant, name, phone, assigned_category_ids,
  can_view_margins: boolean, is_active)
- stock_adjustments (id, variant_id, quantity_delta, reason: restock/damage/correction,
  new_cost_price (nullable), adjusted_by_staff_id, created_at)
- audit_log (id, actor_id, action, target_table, target_id, previous_value, new_value,
  created_at)
- notifications (id, type: new_order/payment_received/low_stock/order_needs_action/
  new_review/cash_discrepancy, message, target_user_id, target_role, read: boolean, created_at)

BUILD IN PHASES

Phase 1 — Foundation
- Supabase schema + RLS policies per role above
- Auth for owner, staff (email/password), and customers (phone OTP or email)
- PWA setup: manifest, installable, offline shell for the storefront

Phase 2 — Storefront & catalog
- Public product catalog: browse by category, filter (size/colour/price), search, product
  detail page with image gallery and variant (size/colour) picker showing live stock
  ("In Stock"/"Low Stock"/"Sold Out")
- Owner/sales-manager product & variant management: create products, add variants, upload
  images, set cost and selling price per variant — auto-generate a unique barcode per
  variant on creation, with a printable label view (name, size/colour, price, barcode)

Phase 3 — Cart, checkout & payments
- Cart (guest and logged-in), wishlist, "browse more" related-items strip
- Checkout: delivery method (courier with zone-based fee, or pickup), payment method
  (M-Pesa STK Push primary, card fallback, Cash on Delivery as an owner-toggleable option
  per delivery zone)
- Order confirmation, SMS/email at each order-status change, printable/shareable receipt

Phase 4 — In-person "quick sale" mode & stock management
- Camera-based barcode scanning: scan a variant's barcode, add to a running in-person sale
  cart, checkout the same way as online (cash/M-Pesa/card), stock decrements automatically
  — usable by sales_attendant and sales_manager roles at a physical stall/counter
- Restock flow: scan or select a variant, enter quantity received and optionally a new cost
  price, stock increments; manual stock_adjustment flow for damage/loss/correction

Phase 5 — Owner dashboard: profit, stock value & sales team
- Profit margin report: cost vs. selling price per variant, gross margin %, total profit —
  filterable by date range, product, category, or store-wide; owner-only by default
- Stock valuation: total cost-basis and retail-basis value of current inventory
- Sales-team management: owner adds/edits sales_manager and sales_attendant accounts,
  assigns categories, sets discount limits, toggles margin visibility per manager
- Per-staff performance dashboard: sales value, orders/transactions handled, average order
  value, any voided sales — visible to owner for appraisal

Phase 6 — Reviews, notifications, audit
- Product reviews/ratings, moderation queue for the owner
- Notification center: new orders, payments received, low stock, orders needing action,
  new reviews, cash discrepancies from in-person sales
- Full audit log: every price change, stock adjustment, discount override, and voided/
  refunded order, with who/when — never hard-delete an order, only void/refund with a reason

Phase 7 — Hardening & deployment
- RLS review: sales_attendant must never see cost prices or margins; a sales_manager without
  can_view_margins must not either; a customer must only ever see their own orders
- Seed demo products across all categories with variants for testing
- Deploy to Vercel; confirm PWA installs correctly on Android/iOS; confirm the barcode
  scanner works reliably under real store lighting, not just a demo environment

DESIGN
Storefront should feel like a real fashion brand, not a spreadsheet: full-bleed product
photography, generous whitespace, smooth image transitions and a satisfying add-to-cart
animation (Framer Motion). Owner/staff dashboards should be the opposite — dense, fast,
legible, large tap targets for the in-store barcode scan flow.

Do not hardcode categories, sizes, or colours anywhere — these are owner-editable, per the
requirement to keep adding more kinds of items (bags, caps, and whatever comes next) without
a code change.

Confirm the plan with me before writing code, then build phase by phase, committing to
GitHub and pushing schema migrations to Supabase as you go.

7. Standalone budget
